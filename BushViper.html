Genus Atheris: The African Bush Viper

Atheris is a genus of venomous vipers found only in tropical sub-Saharan Africa. In an example of convergent evolution, they show many similarities to the arboreal pit vipers of Asia and South America.

Physical Description

They are relatively small in size, with adults ranging from 55 cm for A. katangensis up to 78 cm for A. squamigera. All species have a broad, triangular head distinct from the neck and large eyes with elliptical pupils.

Scale & Body Anatomy

The body is slender, tapering, and slightly compressed with 133–175 rounded ventral scales.

Dorsal & Head Scale Details

Midbody there are 14–36 rows of overlapping dorsal scales with apical pits.

Prehensile Tail Features

The tail is strongly prehensile, featuring 38–67 single subcaudal scales that support the body while suspended from branches.

Microscopic Keeled Ridges

The dorsal scales are strongly keeled, giving the species their characteristic textured appearance.

Herpetology Terms

Arboreal
Arboreal animals are creatures that spend most or all of their lives living, eating, and sleeping in the branches and canopy of tree
Prehensile
Prehensile is an adjective that describes a body part adapted for seizing, grasping, or holding onto objects, usually by wrapping around them
Subcaudal
Subcaudal means situated on the underside, or bottom side, of an animal's tail.
